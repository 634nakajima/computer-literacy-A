## 1. Gmail — Email Basics

At university, Gmail is used to communicate with instructors and staff. Here you'll learn how to **set up a signature** and **write formal emails**.

### 1-1. Log in to Gmail

1. Open [Gmail](https://mail.google.com/) in your browser
2. Log in with your university Google account (`i[student-id]@st.t-kougei.ac.jp`)

::: tip Can't log in?
If you don't know your university email address or password, check the Information Center's guide.
:::

### 1-2. Set up your email signature

A signature is your contact information that is automatically appended to every email you send. It's standard practice in professional communication.

**Setup steps:**

1. Click the **gear icon** ⚙️ in the top right of Gmail
2. Click **"See all settings"**
3. Scroll down in the **"General"** tab to the **"Signature"** section
4. Click **"+ Create new"** and enter a name (e.g., "University")
5. Enter a signature like this:

```
Tokyo Polytechnic University, Faculty of Arts
Dept. of Interactive Media, 1st Year
Student ID: 2024000
Taro Kogei
mail: i2024000@st.t-kougei.ac.jp
```

6. Under "Signature defaults," select this signature for both "For new emails" and "On reply/forward"
7. Click **"Save Changes"** at the bottom of the page

::: warning Note
Replace the student ID, name, and email address with your own information.
:::

### 1-3. Write and send a formal email

You'll often need to email instructors at university. Learning proper email etiquette now will serve you well in your career too.

**Email structure:**

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **To** | `m.nakajima@int.t-kougei.ac.jp` |
| **Subject** | `Assignment 1 (2024000 Taro Kogei)` |
| **Body** | See below |

**Example body:**

```
Dear Professor Nakajima,

I hope this email finds you well.
My name is Taro Kogei, a first-year student
in the Department of Interactive Media.

I don't have much experience using computers,
so I look forward to learning in this class.
Thank you very much for your guidance.

(Your signature will appear automatically below)
```

**Tips for formal emails:**

- Start with a **greeting** and the recipient's title
- **Introduce yourself** with your department and name
- Keep the body **concise and polite**
- End with a **closing phrase** such as "Thank you for your guidance"

::: details Common mistakes to avoid
- Empty subject line → Always include your topic and name in the subject
- Being too casual → Use polite, professional language with instructors
- Jumping straight to the point → Don't forget a greeting and self-introduction
- No signature → Always include your contact information
:::

## 3. Typing Practice

Speed and accuracy on the keyboard are essential for using a PC effectively. Start practicing today and improve gradually over time.

### 3-1. Practice typing on e-typing

1. Go to [e-typing Romaji Typing](https://www.e-typing.ne.jp/roma/check/)
2. Start the **"Level Check"** test
3. Type the displayed text as accurately as possible
4. Check your **score** and **level** on the results screen

**Score guide:**

| Level | Score | Description |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| E–D | 0–100 | Keep practicing! A little every day goes a long way |
| C | 100–200 | Average level. Focus on accuracy |
| B | 200–300 | Good job! Now work on increasing speed |
| A+ | 300+ | Excellent! Keep pushing your limits |

### 3-2. Record your best score

1. Practice several times and note your **best score**
2. Add the following to your **self-introduction Word document**:
   - Typing score
   - Level (E–S)
   - Your thoughts and goals for improvement

::: tip Tips for improving your typing
- Keep your fingers on the **home position** (left index on `F`, right index on `J`)
- Prioritize **accuracy over speed** at first
- Practice **5–10 minutes every day** for the best results
- Aim for **touch typing** — typing without looking at the keyboard
:::

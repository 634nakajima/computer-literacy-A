# Lesson 4: Writing a Report with AI

## Goals for This Lesson

- Understand the difference between a report and an essay
- Experience the report **thinking process** through dialogue with AI
- Learn the flow: theme selection → structure design → arguments and evidence → drafting
- Complete the report as a take-home assignment

---

## 1. What Is a Report?

### 1-1. Essays vs. Reports

| | Personal Essay | Academic Report |
|--|----------------|----------------|
| **Purpose** | Express what you felt | Convince the reader with analysis |
| **Subject** | "I felt that…" | "It can be argued that…" |
| **Evidence** | Optional | **Required** (data, examples, comparisons) |
| **Structure** | Free-form | Logical flow: intro → body → conclusion |
| **Style** | Casual tone OK | Formal, consistent tone |

### 1-2. Report Writing in the AI Era

AI is good at writing text. However, deciding **what to write about, what to argue, and what evidence to use** is a human responsibility.

In this lesson, instead of memorizing formatting rules, we'll take an approach of **experiencing the thinking process of report writing through dialogue with AI**.

```
Traditional: Memorize 21 rules → Can't remember → Nothing sticks

This lesson: Think through dialogue with AI → Experience the process
            → Let AI handle formatting → Thinking skills remain
```

::: tip Do I really not need to memorize formatting rules?
Reports do have formatting standards (see the appendix at the bottom of this page). However, you can **give these rules to AI and ask it to apply them automatically**. What matters most is **thinking about what to write**.
:::

## 2. The Report Assignment

### Theme

> **Choose a media work that has most influenced you (game, video, music, app, web service, etc.), analyze its interaction design, and argue why it captivates people.**

### Requirements

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Topic** | Choose one media work that strongly influenced you |
| **Content** | Not just opinions — include **analysis** and **arguments** |
| **Length** | Word document, 3–5 pages in reading view |
| **Visuals** | Include at least one screenshot or figure |
| **References** | Cite at least one reference source |
| **Submission** | Submit as instructed in class |
| **Deadline** | Day before the next class |

::: warning "I like it" is not enough
"I like this work. It's fun." is an essay. A report requires you to analyze **why** it's engaging and **what mechanisms** captivate people, supported by evidence.
:::

## 3. Build Your Report with AI (In-Class Exercise)

Today, you'll use **Claude** to interactively construct the framework of your report.

### 3-1. Set Up the "Report Writing Companion AI"

1. Log in to [Claude](https://claude.ai/)
2. Select **"Projects"** from the left sidebar (if available on your plan)
3. Create a new project and paste the following template into the project instructions:

**Companion AI instruction template:**
[report-writing-skill.md](https://634nakajima.github.io/computer-literacy-A/report-writing-skill.md)

Copy the text from the link above and paste it into Claude's project instructions (or at the beginning of your chat).

::: tip If you can't use the Projects feature
Paste the template content as your first message in a regular chat, then tell Claude: "Please follow these instructions to help me write my report."
:::

### 3-2. Phase 1 — Choose Your Theme (10–15 min)

Tell the AI "Let's begin" and it will start asking questions like:

> "What media work have you recently been so absorbed in that you lost track of time?"

**What to do:**
- Honestly share a work you truly love
- Answer the AI's follow-up questions in your own words
- Choose the analysis angle that feels most natural to you

::: details Examples of works you might choose
- Mobile games (Genshin Impact, Project SEKAI, Pokémon GO, etc.)
- Console games (Zelda, Minecraft, Animal Crossing, Splatoon, etc.)
- Social media/apps (TikTok, Instagram, Spotify, LINE, etc.)
- Video content (Netflix shows, YouTubers, music videos, anime, etc.)
- Music experiences (live streaming, rhythm games, AI playlists, etc.)
- Web services (Canva, Notion, Pinterest, Figma, etc.)
:::

### 3-3. Phase 2 — Design the Structure (10–15 min)

Once your theme is decided, the AI will propose a report outline.

**What to do:**
- Read the proposed structure and consider: "Will this flow make sense to a reader?"
- Tell the AI if you want to change anything
- Check that your **introduction** poses a question that your **conclusion** answers

### 3-4. Phase 3 — Develop Arguments and Evidence (20–25 min)

This is the most important phase. You'll discuss each section with the AI.

**The AI will ask you:**
- "What's the main point you want to make in this section?"
- "What evidence supports that claim?"
- "Can you point to a specific scene or feature that demonstrates this?"

**What to do:**
- Answer in your own words (it's OK if it's rough)
- The AI will rephrase your ideas in academic language — check if the meaning is preserved
- If you can't think of evidence, say so — the AI will provide hints and help with research

::: info Types of "evidence"
- **Specific examples**: "In this game, every time you defeat an enemy, there's an effect and sound"
- **Comparisons**: "Similar game A lacks this feature, but game B has it — that difference affects the experience"
- **Data**: "This title sold 10 million copies within its first year"
- **Theory/concepts**: "According to flow theory, the balance between challenge and skill creates immersion"
:::

### 3-5. Phase 4 — Generate a Draft (10–15 min)

Based on your Phase 3 discussions, the AI generates a draft section by section.

**What to do:**
- Read each draft section and check: "Does this match what I want to say?"
- Request changes for anything that doesn't feel right
- Once all sections are ready, read through the entire report

### 3-6. Phase 5 — Export the Dialogue Summary

Finally, tell the AI: "Please output the dialogue summary." This produces:

- Your chosen work and theme
- Report structure with section summaries
- Arguments and evidence for each section
- What you discovered through the dialogue

This summary serves as your blueprint for finishing the report outside of class.

## 4. Take-Home Assignment — Complete the Report

### 4-1. What to Do

1. **Rewrite the draft in your own words**
   - Don't just copy-paste the AI output — make it sound like your own writing
   - Your perspective in the "Discussion" section is especially important
2. **Add figures**
   - Include at least one screenshot or image related to the work
   - Add a number and title below each figure (e.g., "Figure 1: The game's main interface")
3. **Add references**
   - List sources cited in your report at the end
4. **Polish the formatting**
   - You may give Claude the formatting rules (see appendix) and ask it to check your report
   - Apply the Word formatting skills learned in Lesson 3

### 4-2. What to Submit

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Report** (Word document) | 3–5 pages with figures and references |
| 2 | **Claude conversation share link** | Get the URL using Claude's share feature |

::: tip How to share your Claude conversation
1. In the Claude chat, click the **share button** (↗ icon) in the top right
2. Click "Create link"
3. Copy the generated URL and submit it

※ If the share feature isn't available, attach the "Dialogue Summary" from Phase 5 at the end of your Word document instead.
:::

::: warning Pre-submission checklist
- [ ] Does your report contain **analysis and arguments** (not just opinions)?
- [ ] Is each argument supported by **evidence** (examples, comparisons, data)?
- [ ] Does the **conclusion** answer the question posed in the **introduction**?
- [ ] Are **figures** included with numbers and titles?
- [ ] Are **references** listed?
- [ ] Is the writing style formal and consistent?
- [ ] Is the Claude conversation link (or dialogue summary) included?
:::

## Summary

In this lesson, you learned **how to use AI as a thinking partner for report writing**:

1. **Theme selection** — Find an analysis angle from a media work you love
2. **Structure design** — Build a logical structure with AI that will convince readers
3. **Arguments and evidence** — Rigorously ask "why can I say this?"
4. **Draft generation** — Let AI write, then make it your own

> Using AI is not "cheating." What matters is **thinking about what needs to be written**. AI is a tool for articulating and organizing your thoughts. With strong thinking skills, you can write a report on any topic.

## Appendix: Report Formatting Rules

The following rules are the basics of report formatting. You can give these to Claude with the instruction "Check my report against these rules" for automatic review.

### Format Rules

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| 1 | Use **sans-serif font** (e.g., Yu Gothic) for headings, **serif font** (e.g., Yu Mincho) for body |
| 2 | Keep font type and size **consistent** throughout |
| 3 | **Indent** the first line of each paragraph |

### Writing Style Rules

| # | Rule | Bad Example | Good Example |
|---|------|-------------|--------------|
| 4 | Use **"the author"** instead of "I" | I found that… | The author found that… |
| 5 | Use **formal, consistent** tone | I think it's cool | It can be considered noteworthy |
| 6 | Avoid **subjective expressions** | It was really amazing | It has received high acclaim |
| 7 | Avoid **colloquial language** | I kinda think that… | This suggests that… |
| 8 | Avoid **vague expressions** | It feels like maybe… | It is possible that… |
| 9 | Avoid **incomplete sentences** | About the usability. | The usability is discussed below. |
| 10 | Don't **ask questions** in the text | Have you ever wondered…? | It is known that… |

### Structure Rules

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| 11 | Start each paragraph with a **topic sentence** |
| 12 | In the conclusion, **review the entire report** and state your findings |
| 13 | Write as **objective analysis**, not personal reflection |

### Figures & Citations

| # | Rule | Bad | Good |
|---|------|-----|------|
| 14 | Add **number and title** below figures | Chart 1 | Figure 1: Average haptic magnitude |
| 15 | **Reference figures** in the body text | (figure with no mention) | Figure 1 shows… |
| 16 | Add **citation numbers** at reference points | …is known. | …is known [1]. |
| 17 | Create a **reference list** at the end | [1] Paper title | [1] Author. "Title", Source, Year. |
